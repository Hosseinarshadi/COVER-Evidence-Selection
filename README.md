# COVER-Evidence-Selection
Replication materials for a study of how AI selects investigative evidence and when it fails to defer, in AI-assisted threat hunting.

AI assistants increasingly decide which evidence professionals examine, not only what they conclude. Because an analyst can contest advice they see but not a check they are never shown, this upstream delegation, which we call delegated evidence attention, can escape oversight that focuses on AI outputs. This repository supports a matched-state computational experiment that examines it in threat hunting. In each decision state, a language model (claude-haiku-5-5) generates competing benign and malicious explanations, self-reported probabilities and predicted outcomes for a fixed catalog of eight read-only checks; these inputs are held constant while different selectors choose the next check: LLM-led selection (which may decline), expected information gain per cost, a label-blind fixed order, chance, and COVER (Counterfactual and COmpeting-hypothesis eVidence rEcommendeR), which ranks checks that challenge the leading hypothesis and excludes verifier-rejected ones, with and without a restraint rule that withholds a recommendation when no verifier-supported challenge exists. Selections are scored against a scorer-only evidence map fixed before the held-out run, in which a check qualifies when its observed outcome is at least twice as likely under the true label as under the alternative. Experiment E1 measures the share of non-redundant diagnostic selections in 40 decision states and recommendation persistence when all qualifying checks are masked; experiment E2 runs 320 sequential investigations (20 cases × eight conditions × two repetitions, up to three checks each) to compare verdicts, including conditions in which a wrong analyst hunch is shown to or hidden from the hypothesis generator. The held-out cases are 20 recorded alerts (10 attacks, 10 benign look-alikes from eight simulated attack scenarios) sampled from the public AIT Alert Data Set; cases are the unit of inference, with case-clustered bootstrap confidence intervals (2,000 resamples, fixed seed). The repository also includes the development comparison that preceded the held-out study: six constructed scenarios in which the model-led and information-gain selectors were compared at two states each, five repetitions per state (60 decisions). All scenarios and cases are synthetic or drawn from simulated intrusions; no live security operations data or human participants are involved.
